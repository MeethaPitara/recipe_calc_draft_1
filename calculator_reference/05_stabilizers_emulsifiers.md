# 05 — Stabilizers and Emulsifiers

## Overview
Stabilizers and emulsifiers are used in small quantities (typically 0.2-0.8% total) but have outsized impact on texture, meltdown, shelf stability, and air retention. Understanding their distinct roles is critical — stabilizers and emulsifiers do fundamentally different things despite often being sold as blended "stab-emul" systems.

---

## Stabilizers (Hydrocolloids)

### Core function
Stabilizers are long-chain molecules (polysaccharides or proteins) that bind water and increase the viscosity of the unfrozen phase. They do NOT prevent ice crystal formation — they slow ice crystal GROWTH during storage.

### How they work
1. **Water binding:** Stabilizer molecules attract and hold water molecules, reducing the amount of "free" water available to migrate and feed growing ice crystals.
2. **Viscosity increase:** By thickening the unfrozen liquid phase between ice crystals, stabilizers physically slow the rate at which water molecules can move from small crystals to large crystals (a process called Ostwald ripening or recrystallization).
3. **Melt resistance:** A more viscous unfrozen phase drains more slowly as ice melts, giving the product a slower, more controlled meltdown.

### What stabilizers do NOT do
- They do not prevent initial ice crystal formation during freezing.
- They do not change the freezing point.
- They do not change sweetness, fat content, or other compositional metrics significantly (their contribution to total solids is real but small at typical dosing).
- They cannot fix a fundamentally unbalanced recipe — they can improve a decent recipe's texture, but they cannot rescue a recipe with 70% water.

### Common Stabilizers Used in Gelato

#### Locust Bean Gum (LBG / Carob Bean Gum)
- **E number:** E410.
- **Typical dosing:** 0.1-0.3%.
- **Behavior:** Synergistic with carrageenan and xanthan. Provides excellent melt resistance. Creates a smooth, slightly chewy texture. Requires heat to fully hydrate (>80°C). Works well with dairy proteins.
- **Best for:** Primary stabilizer in gelato. Good all-around performer.

#### Guar Gum
- **E number:** E412.
- **Typical dosing:** 0.1-0.3%.
- **Behavior:** Hydrates in cold water (unlike LBG). Very effective thickener. Can create a gummy/slimy texture if overdosed. Less synergy with other gums than LBG.
- **Best for:** Cost-effective thickening. Cold-process applications. Often blended with LBG.

#### Carrageenan (Kappa or Lambda)
- **E number:** E407.
- **Typical dosing:** 0.01-0.03% (very low — it is used as a secondary stabilizer).
- **Behavior:** Interacts with milk proteins (specifically casein) to prevent whey separation. At high doses, creates a rigid, brittle gel — so dosing must be conservative.
- **Critical role:** Prevents the "wheying off" defect where the mix separates into a watery phase and a protein-rich phase.
- **Caution:** Overdosing carrageenan is one of the most common stabilizer errors. At 0.05%+ in a milk-based system, it can create a rubbery, body-less texture.

#### Xanthan Gum
- **E number:** E415.
- **Typical dosing:** 0.02-0.05%.
- **Behavior:** Extreme pseudoplasticity (shear-thinning) — thick when still, thin when flowing. Good freeze-thaw stability. Synergistic with LBG (the combination gels, which neither does alone at low concentration).
- **Best for:** Freeze-thaw resistance in delivery products (relevant for Zomato cold chain).

#### CMC (Carboxymethyl Cellulose / Cellulose Gum)
- **E number:** E466.
- **Typical dosing:** 0.1-0.2%.
- **Behavior:** Good general-purpose stabilizer. Hydrates cold. Less synergy than LBG blends. Can taste slightly "cellulose-y" if overdosed.
- **Best for:** Simple formulations where a single stabilizer is needed.

#### Tara Gum
- **E number:** E417.
- **Typical dosing:** 0.1-0.2%.
- **Behavior:** Between LBG and guar in properties. Partially cold-soluble. Good synergy with xanthan and carrageenan.
- **Best for:** Alternative to LBG when LBG is unavailable or cost-prohibitive.

### Stabilizer Blending Principles

#### Why blends work better than single stabilizers
No single stabilizer excels at every function. Blends are designed so that each component covers a different aspect:
- LBG for melt resistance and smooth texture.
- Guar for viscosity and cold-hydration insurance.
- Carrageenan (tiny amount) for protein stabilization.
- Xanthan (tiny amount) for freeze-thaw stability.

#### Typical gelato stabilizer blend (total: 0.3-0.5% of mix)
- LBG: 50-60% of the blend.
- Guar: 20-30% of the blend.
- Carrageenan: 5-8% of the blend.
- Xanthan or CMC: balance.

#### Commercial blends
MeethaPitara may use a pre-formulated stabilizer blend from a supplier (e.g., Cremodan, Palsgaard). In this case, the blend composition is proprietary but the total dosing recommendation from the supplier should be followed. The agent should treat the blend as a single ingredient with a known total solids contribution and an effective stabilization level.

---

### Stabilizer Dosing: How to Adjust

#### Signs of under-stabilization
- Icy texture after 2+ weeks of storage.
- Fast, liquid meltdown (product melts into a puddle quickly).
- Visible ice crystals on the surface after temperature cycling.
- Whey separation (if carrageenan is specifically lacking).

#### Signs of over-stabilization
- Gummy, chewy, or slimy texture.
- Product feels "heavy" or "pasty" on the palate despite correct fat/solids levels.
- Meltdown is TOO slow — product sits as a dome for a long time without melting, which seems good but actually indicates gel-like behavior that consumers find unnatural.
- Rubbery or bouncy texture (carrageenan overdose specifically).

## Emulsifiers

### Core function (counterintuitive)
In ice cream/gelato, emulsifiers do the opposite of what their name suggests. They DESTABILIZE the fat emulsion — specifically, they displace proteins from the surface of fat globules, making the fat more prone to partial coalescence during freezing.

### Why partial coalescence is desirable
1. When the mix enters the continuous freezer, fat globules are being sheared by the dasher while the mix freezes.
2. If proteins are firmly coating the fat globules (no emulsifier), the globules stay separate — they don't connect.
3. With emulsifier present, proteins are displaced, and fat globules can partially merge (coalesce) when they collide during shearing.
4. These partially coalesced fat clusters form a network that:
   - **Surrounds and stabilizes air cells** — air incorporated during freezing is held in place by fat.
   - **Gives the product "dryness"** — the mix at the extruder exit stands up instead of slumping.
   - **Creates shape retention** — scoops hold their form.
   - **Provides smooth mouthfeel** — the fat network breaks down smoothly during eating.

### What happens without emulsifier
- Product is wet and soupy at the extruder.
- Poor air retention — overrun drops and is unstable.
- Fast, liquid meltdown.
- Less smooth texture.

### What happens with too much emulsifier
- Excessive fat destabilization → fat churning (visible butter grains in the mix).
- Greasy mouthfeel.
- Reduced creaminess (paradoxically, over-churned fat doesn't distribute smoothly).

---

### Common Emulsifiers

#### Mono- and Diglycerides (E471)
- Most common ice cream emulsifier.
- Typical dosing: 0.2-0.5%.
- Effective protein displacers. Well-understood behavior.
- Often sold as distilled monoglycerides (GMS — glyceryl monostearate) for purity.

#### Polysorbate 80 (Tween 80) (E433)
- Very effective protein displacer — more aggressive than mono/diglycerides.
- Typical dosing: 0.02-0.10%.
- Creates very dry, stiff extrudate.
- Used in combination with mono/diglycerides, not alone.
- Lower doses needed than mono/diglycerides.

#### Egg Yolk Lecithin
- Natural emulsifier from egg yolks.
- NOT used in MeethaPitara formulations (egg-free positioning).
- Mentioned for reference: provides moderate emulsification + fat + protein + flavor.

#### Soy Lecithin
- Plant-based emulsifier.
- Weaker protein displacer than mono/diglycerides or polysorbate 80.
- Sometimes used in clean-label or vegan formulations.
- Less effective for air stabilization in dairy gelato.

---

### Emulsifier Dosing: How to Adjust

#### Signs of under-emulsification
- Wet, drippy product at extrusion.
- Overrun falls short of target.
- Fast, uncontrolled meltdown.
- Product doesn't hold scoop shape.

#### Signs of over-emulsification
- Visible fat grains or butter flecks.
- Greasy, tallowy mouthfeel.
- Product feels "waxy" on the palate.
- Ironically, very over-emulsified product can melt faster because the fat network is over-structured and collapses suddenly rather than gradually.

---

## Stab-Emul System: Putting It Together

### Typical total stab-emul dosing for gelato
- Total stabilizer: 0.3-0.5% of mix.
- Total emulsifier: 0.2-0.4% of mix.
- Combined: 0.5-0.8% of mix.

### Important for the agent
- Commercial stab-emul blends combine stabilizers and emulsifiers in a single product. The agent should treat these as a single ingredient but understand that both functions are present.
- When troubleshooting, it helps to know the blend composition to diagnose whether the stabilizer component or the emulsifier component is the issue.
- Stab-emul contributes to "other solids" in the total solids calculation. At 0.5-0.8%, this is a small but non-negligible contribution.
