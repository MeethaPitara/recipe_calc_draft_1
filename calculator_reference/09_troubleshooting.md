# 09 — Troubleshooting: Defect → Cause → Fix

## Overview
This document maps common gelato and ice cream defects to their likely causes and recommended fixes. The agent should use this as a diagnostic tool: when a sensory or quality defect is reported, trace it through the cause chains below and suggest the most likely formulation or process adjustment.

---

## Texture Defects

### Defect: Icy / Coarse Texture
**What the person reports:** "Feels gritty," "ice crystals on tongue," "rough texture," "coarse."

**Likely causes (check in order):**
1. **Too much free water.** Total solids too low → too much water available to form large crystals.
   - Fix: Increase total solids (add SMP, sugar, or maltodextrin).
2. **Insufficient stabilizer.** Not enough water binding → free water migrates and forms large crystals during storage.
   - Fix: Increase stabilizer dose (especially LBG or guar).
3. **Slow hardening.** Blast freezer not cold enough or product too large for the freezer capacity.
   - Fix: Ensure blast at -35°C to -40°C. Check load — don't overload the blast freezer.
4. **Temperature abuse during storage/distribution.** Freeze-thaw cycles grow crystals.
   - Fix: Improve cold chain. Increase stabilizer for more freeze-thaw resilience. Add xanthan to the stabilizer blend.
5. **Insufficient aging.** Fat didn't crystallize properly → poor fat network → poor air stability → icy.
   - Fix: Age minimum 4 hours at 4°C, ideally 12-24 hours.
6. **Low overrun.** Very dense product with no air insulation → ice crystals feel more prominent.
   - Fix: Increase overrun target if appropriate for the product positioning.

---

### Defect: Sandy / Gritty Texture (different from icy)
**What the person reports:** "Sandy feeling," "like fine grains," "gritty but not ice."

**Likely cause:**
1. **Lactose crystallization.** MSNF is too high (above ~12%), causing lactose to exceed its solubility limit in the unfrozen phase and crystallize.
   - Fix: Reduce MSNF. Check all sources of MSNF (milk, SMP, mawa, condensed milk). Pay special attention to mawa-based pastes — the MSNF trap.
2. **Old product.** Lactose crystallization worsens over time in storage.
   - Fix: Reduce MSNF as above. Shorten shelf life claim if needed.

**Critical note for MeethaPitara:** Any recipe using mawa-based pastes (GJ Paste, rasmalai) is at elevated sandiness risk. Always verify total MSNF including paste contribution.

---

### Defect: Gummy / Chewy / Elastic Texture
**What the person reports:** "Gummy," "chewy like gum," "elastic," "sticky."

**Likely causes:**
1. **Over-stabilization.** Too much stabilizer (especially guar or LBG) creates excessive viscosity and gel-like texture.
   - Fix: Reduce stabilizer dose by 10-20%.
2. **Too much glucose syrup (low DE).** Glucose syrup oligosaccharides add body and chewiness.
   - Fix: Reduce glucose syrup. Replace with sucrose or dextrose.
3. **Very high total solids.** Above 42%, the product becomes too dense and chewy.
   - Fix: Reduce total solids (usually by reducing SMP, sugar, or other solids).

---

### Defect: Rubbery / Bouncy Texture
**What the person reports:** "Rubbery," "bounces back when pressed," "unnatural."

**Likely cause:**
1. **Carrageenan overdose.** Kappa-carrageenan at >0.04% in a dairy system gels with casein, creating rigidity.
   - Fix: Reduce carrageenan to 0.01-0.03%. This is a very common mistake because carrageenan is dosed at much lower levels than other stabilizers.

---

### Defect: Heavy / Dense Texture
**What the person reports:** "Too heavy," "feels like eating butter," "sits in stomach."

**Likely causes:**
1. **Fat too high.** Above 10% in gelato, product feels heavy.
   - Fix: Reduce fat. Target 6-8% for gelato.
2. **Overrun too low.** Product is too dense because not enough air.
   - Fix: Increase overrun (adjust CF air injection or check emulsifier level).
3. **Total solids too high.** Above 42%, everything is concentrated and heavy.
   - Fix: Reduce solids.

## Softness / Hardness Defects

### Defect: Too Soft at Serving Temperature
**What the person reports:** "Soupy," "can't hold a scoop," "melts immediately," "too soft."

**Likely causes:**
1. **PAC too high.** Too much freezing point depression → too much unfrozen water at serving temp.
   - Fix: Replace high-PAC sugars (dextrose, invert) with lower-PAC sugars (sucrose, glucose syrup). See sugar document.
2. **Too much total sugar.** Even if PAC per gram is moderate, excessive total sugar increases total PAC.
   - Fix: Reduce total sugar.
3. **Serving temperature too warm.** If display/storage is at -10°C instead of -14°C.
   - Fix: Adjust serving temp, or reformulate for the actual serving temperature.
4. **Unexpected sugar contributions.** Paste, condensed milk, gulkand, or other ingredients adding sugar that wasn't accounted for in the PAC calculation.
   - Fix: Audit all sugar sources. Recalculate PAC including all contributions.

---

### Defect: Too Hard at Serving Temperature
**What the person reports:** "Can't scoop," "rock hard," "have to wait 10 minutes before eating."

**Likely causes:**
1. **PAC too low.** Not enough freezing point depression → too much ice at serving temp.
   - Fix: Replace some sucrose with dextrose (high PAC). Or add a small amount of invert sugar.
2. **Serving temperature too cold.** Home freezers at -22°C are much colder than gelato display at -12°C.
   - Fix: For D2C/delivery products, target PAC at the higher end of the range (26-30) to compensate for cold home freezers.
3. **Low total sugar.** Below 16%, insufficient freezing point depression.
   - Fix: Increase sugar.

**Critical for MeethaPitara:** Since products are sold via Zomato for home consumption (eaten from home freezers, not display cases), hardness at home freezer temperatures is a real and frequent issue. PAC should be calibrated for -18°C, not -12°C.

## Flavor Defects

### Defect: Weak / Muted Flavor
**Likely causes:**
1. **High overrun.** Air dilutes flavor.
   - Fix: Reduce overrun or increase flavoring dose.
2. **Fat masking.** High fat coats the palate and prevents water-soluble flavors from reaching taste receptors quickly.
   - Fix: Reduce fat slightly. Increase flavor dose. Use more intense flavor compounds.
3. **Insufficient flavoring.** Simply not enough flavor compound.
   - Fix: Increase paste or extract dosing.
4. **Flavor lost during processing.** Volatile compounds destroyed by pasteurization.
   - Fix: Add heat-sensitive flavors post-pasteurization.
5. **Product too cold.** Flavor perception drops as temperature drops. Rock-hard product releases flavor poorly.
   - Fix: Reformulate for better scoopability (higher PAC) so the product softens faster in the mouth.

---

### Defect: Off-Flavors — Cooked / Scorched
**Likely causes:**
1. **Excessive pasteurization.** Temperature too high or hold time too long.
   - Fix: Check pasteurization temp/time. 85°C for HTST is standard; don't exceed.
2. **Maillard browning from SMP.** High-heat SMP already has some cooked flavor; further pasteurization amplifies it.
   - Fix: Use low-heat SMP if available.

---

### Defect: Off-Flavors — Rancid / Soapy
**Likely causes:**
1. **Lipase activity.** Enzymes breaking down fat into free fatty acids. Common in raw milk products not properly pasteurized.
   - Fix: Ensure pasteurization is adequate. Don't add raw milk/cream after pasteurization.
2. **Old ingredients.** Ghee or cream past their prime.
   - Fix: Check ingredient freshness. Ghee should smell nutty, not sharp.

---

### Defect: Off-Flavors — Soapy (specifically)
**In the context of kewra or rose flavoring:**
1. **Overdosed kewra.** Kewra extract is extremely concentrated. Even a few extra drops can shift from "floral" to "soapy."
   - Fix: Reduce kewra by 30-50%. Start with minimal doses and scale up.

## Meltdown Defects

### Defect: Fast, Watery Meltdown
**Likely causes:**
1. **Low fat.** Insufficient fat network.
2. **Insufficient emulsifier.** Poor partial coalescence → weak fat structure.
3. **Under-stabilized.** Low viscosity unfrozen phase drains quickly.
4. **Low total solids.** Too much free water.
5. **Insufficient aging.** Fat not crystallized, emulsifiers not adsorbed.
- **Fix:** Systematically check fat%, emulsifier dose, stabilizer dose, total solids, and aging time.

---

### Defect: No Melt / Too Slow Melt
**Likely causes:**
1. **Over-stabilization.** Gel-like unfrozen phase doesn't flow.
2. **Over-emulsification.** Extremely rigid fat network.
3. **Very high total solids.** Product is so dense it doesn't flow.
- **Fix:** Reduce stabilizer and/or emulsifier. Check if total solids are above 42%.

---

## Process-Related Defects

### Defect: Visible Butter Grains / Fat Specks
**Likely cause:** Fat has churned in the CF due to excessive shear + high fat + high emulsifier.
- **Fix:** Reduce dasher speed, reduce emulsifier, or reduce fat.

### Defect: Shrinkage (product pulls away from container walls during storage)
**Likely cause:** Overrun is unstable — air leaks out during hardening/storage. Usually caused by insufficient fat coalescence (air cells aren't stabilized).
- **Fix:** Check emulsifier level. Check aging time. Verify overrun stability by weighing product at different time points.

### Defect: Whey Separation (liquid layer on surface)
**Likely cause:** Insufficient carrageenan. Milk proteins are not stabilized, and the aqueous phase separates.
- **Fix:** Add or increase carrageenan (0.01-0.03%).

## Sodium Problem (Mumbai Chaat Surprise context)

### The specific issue
During development of the Mumbai Chaat Surprise sorbet, a sodium problem was identified with Mother's Recipe tamarind paste. Commercial tamarind pastes often contain added salt or sodium benzoate as a preservative. In a sorbet (which has no dairy fat/protein to buffer flavors), even small amounts of sodium can create a noticeably salty or off-putting taste.

### Diagnosis approach
- Taste the tamarind paste neat. If it tastes salty, it has added sodium.
- Check the ingredient list for: salt, sodium benzoate (E211), sodium metabisulfite (E223).
- Even preservative-level sodium (~0.1-0.3%) in the paste, when the paste is used at 5-10% of the mix, can create a perceptible salty note.

### Fix
- Source tamarind paste without added salt/sodium preservatives.
- Or: make tamarind concentrate in-house from raw tamarind pulp.
- Or: reduce the dose and compensate with citric acid for sourness.
