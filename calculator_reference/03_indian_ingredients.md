# 03 — Indian Ingredients: Compositions and Behavior

## Overview
MeethaPitara's core differentiation is mithai-inspired gelato, which means the formulator works with Indian dairy products, sweeteners, and flavor compounds that are absent from Western ice cream databases. These ingredients have unique compositions, behaviors, and hidden contributions that must be understood precisely. Getting mawa wrong by a few percent on MSNF can push an entire recipe into the sandiness danger zone.

---

## Mawa (Khoya) — The Most Critical Indian Ingredient

### What mawa is
Mawa is milk that has been slowly reduced (evaporated) by heating in an open pan (kadhai) until most of the water is driven off. It is concentrated whole milk — not a powder, not a reconstituted product. The Maillard browning that occurs during reduction gives mawa its characteristic cooked, caramelized flavor that defines many Indian sweets.

### Why mawa is tricky for formulation
Mawa is NOT just "milk solids." It contains:
- Residual moisture (typically 20-30% depending on type and supplier).
- Milk fat (typically 20-25%).
- Milk proteins and lactose (MSNF) — and this is where the trap lies.

### The MSNF trap — the critical correction
Because mawa is concentrated milk, its MSNF per 100g is much higher than whole milk. Early MeethaPitara formulations underestimated mawa's MSNF contribution at 9.91% per 100g. Lab analysis and recalculation showed the actual contribution is approximately 14.06% per 100g for the specific mawa type used in GJ Paste.

This ~4% error per 100g of mawa, compounded across a recipe using 15-20% paste (which itself contains significant mawa), can push final MSNF well above the 12% sandiness threshold.

### Lesson for the agent
- Never assume mawa composition from generic databases.
- Always use supplier-specific or lab-verified composition data.
- When mawa is part of a paste, compute the paste's MSNF contribution by tracing through: mawa composition → paste composition → final mix composition.
- Flag any recipe where mawa-derived MSNF pushes total MSNF above 11%.

### Types of mawa and their approximate compositions

#### Batti (Pindi) Khoya
- Lowest moisture, hardest texture.
- Moisture: ~18-20%.
- Fat: ~25-28%.
- MSNF: ~14-16% (estimated).
- Used in: barfi, peda, gulab jamun filling.
- Behavior in gelato: contributes most solids per gram, highest sandiness risk, strongest cooked-milk flavor.

#### Dhap (Daap) Khoya
- Medium moisture, granular texture.
- Moisture: ~25-28%.
- Fat: ~20-23%.
- MSNF: ~12-14% (estimated).
- Used in: gulab jamun, kalakand.

#### Chikna (Mava) Khoya
- Highest moisture, soft and spreadable.
- Moisture: ~30-35%.
- Fat: ~18-22%.
- MSNF: ~10-13% (estimated).
- Used in: burfi variants, as a base for further cooking.

### Important: These compositions vary significantly by supplier, milk source (cow vs buffalo), and reduction time. Buffalo milk mawa has higher fat and solids than cow milk mawa.

## Desi Ghee (Clarified Butter)

### What it is
Ghee is butter that has been heated until all moisture evaporates and milk solids separate and brown (giving ghee its nutty aroma). The clear fat is decanted.

### Composition
- Fat: 99.0-99.8%.
- Moisture: <0.5%.
- Milk solids: <0.5% (negligible — unlike butter, ghee contributes essentially zero MSNF).

### Behavior in gelato formulation
- Pure fat source. Adding ghee increases fat % directly with almost no impact on other metrics.
- Ghee is not interchangeable with cream in formulation. Cream brings water and MSNF; ghee brings only fat.
- 10g of ghee ≈ 10g of fat. 10g of 35% cream ≈ 3.5g fat + 6.5g water + small MSNF.
- Ghee flavor is heat-stable and carries through freezing well.

### Special uses
- **Noisette ghee (browned ghee):** Ghee heated past clarification until milk solids brown deeply. Used in Jalebi Paste formulation for deeper flavor. Same composition as regular ghee (the browned solids are strained out).
- **Saffron blooming in ghee:** Fat-soluble saffron compounds (crocin, safranal) dissolve more effectively in warm ghee than in water or milk. This is the method used in GJ Paste v2.2 — saffron is bloomed in ghee before incorporation.

## Jaggery (Gur)

### What it is
Unrefined or semi-refined cane sugar (or palm sugar). Unlike white sugar, jaggery retains molasses, minerals, and invert sugars.

### Composition (approximate, varies widely)
- Sucrose: 65-75%.
- Invert sugars (glucose + fructose): 10-15%.
- Moisture: 5-10%.
- Minerals and other solids: 3-5%.

### Why jaggery is different from sucrose for formulation
- **PAC is higher than pure sucrose** because of the invert sugar content. Glucose and fructose are monosaccharides with higher PAC factors than sucrose (a disaccharide). A recipe using jaggery in place of sucrose at the same weight will be softer at serving temperature.
- **POD is slightly different** — the molasses and mineral content can slightly mask sweetness, but the invert sugars are sweeter per gram than sucrose. Net effect depends on the specific jaggery.
- **Moisture content** means jaggery contributes less total sugar per gram than dry sucrose. 100g of jaggery ≈ 75-85g of actual sugars vs 100g for sucrose.
- **Color and flavor** — jaggery adds deep caramel/molasses notes and brown color. This is desirable in specific SKUs but limits use in "white" or light-colored products.

### Formulation guidance
- When substituting jaggery for sucrose, adjust for: lower sugar purity (reduce effective sugar contribution), higher PAC (from invert sugars), added moisture, and flavor impact.
- Jaggery quality varies enormously. Chemical jaggery (made with lime and synthetic clarifiers) behaves differently from artisanal gur. Composition should be verified per batch if possible.

## Gulkand (Rose Petal Preserve)

### What it is
A traditional Ayurvedic preparation of rose petals preserved in sugar. The petals are layered with sugar (usually granulated) and sun-dried or slow-cooked until a thick, jammy consistency is achieved.

### Composition (approximate)
- Sugar: 60-70% (primarily sucrose).
- Moisture: 15-25%.
- Rose petal solids: 5-10%.
- Other (rose water, sometimes with mukhwas additions like saunf, elaichi): variable.

### Behavior in gelato
- Primarily a sugar and flavor source. Contributes to POD and PAC through its sugar content.
- The high sugar content means gulkand shifts sweetness significantly.
- Rose flavor compounds are volatile and partially water-soluble — some flavor loss during pasteurization is expected.
- The jammy texture can create pockets of unfrozen sugar solution in the final product if not properly dispersed.

### Formulation guidance
- Treat gulkand as a sugar source with flavor. Account for its sugar contribution in the POD/PAC calculation.
- Disperse thoroughly during mixing to avoid sugar pockets.
- Consider adding gulkand post-pasteurization (if food safety allows) to preserve volatile rose aromatics.

## Saffron (Kesar)

### What it is
The dried stigmas of Crocus sativus. The world's most expensive spice by weight.

### Active compounds
- **Crocin:** Water-soluble, responsible for yellow-orange color. Survives pasteurization reasonably well.
- **Safranal:** Volatile, responsible for the distinctive saffron aroma. Partially lost during heating.
- **Picrocrocin:** Responsible for the bitter taste. Heat-stable.

### Behavior in gelato formulation
- Saffron at gelato dosing levels (0.01-0.05% of mix weight) has negligible impact on fat, sugar, MSNF, or total solids. It is a pure flavor/color ingredient at these levels.
- **Blooming method matters enormously.** Saffron bloomed in warm ghee extracts fat-soluble compounds more effectively. Saffron steeped in warm milk or cream extracts water-soluble crocin better. The GJ Paste protocol uses ghee blooming for depth of flavor.
- Color intensity is dose-dependent and also affected by pH (slightly acidic = more orange, neutral/alkaline = more yellow).

## Cardamom (Elaichi)

### Active compounds
- Essential oils (primarily 1,8-cineole and α-terpinyl acetate) — these are volatile.
- Best added late in processing or via cold infusion to preserve aroma.

### Behavior in gelato
- At typical dosing (0.1-0.3%), no meaningful impact on formulation metrics.
- Green cardamom (choti elaichi) is used for sweet applications.
- Flavor is best extracted by grinding just before use — pre-ground cardamom loses volatiles rapidly.
- Pairs naturally with saffron, rose, and mawa flavors.

---

## Kewra (Pandanus / Screwpine Extract)

### What it is
A floral extract distilled from the flowers of Pandanus fascicularis. Used in many North Indian mithais.

### Behavior in gelato
- Pure flavor compound. Zero impact on formulation metrics at typical dosing.
- Highly aromatic — a few drops per kg is sufficient. Easy to overdose, which creates a soapy or artificial taste.
- Volatile — add post-pasteurization if possible.

---

## Condensed Milk (Milkmaid / Other Brands)

### Composition (typical sweetened condensed milk)
- Sugar: ~55%.
- Fat: ~8%.
- MSNF: ~20%.
- Moisture: ~27%.

### Behavior in gelato formulation
- Very concentrated source of BOTH sugar and MSNF. A small amount shifts multiple metrics significantly.
- Using condensed milk as a sweetener also adds substantial MSNF — this is often overlooked.
- 100g of condensed milk adds ~55g sugar AND ~20g MSNF. This dual contribution must be tracked.

## Paneer (Indian Cottage Cheese)

### What it is
Acid-coagulated fresh cheese made from heated milk + lemon juice or vinegar.

### Composition (approximate)
- Fat: 20-25%.
- Protein: 15-18%.
- Moisture: 50-55%.
- Lactose: minimal (most lactose drains out with whey).
- MSNF: ~15-18% (mostly protein, very little lactose — so sandiness risk is lower than mawa at similar MSNF levels because lactose is the sandiness culprit, not protein).

### Behavior in gelato
- Adds body and richness through protein.
- Lower sandiness risk than mawa at comparable MSNF levels because lactose is largely removed.
- Grainy texture of paneer needs thorough processing (blending, homogenization) to avoid graininess in final product.
